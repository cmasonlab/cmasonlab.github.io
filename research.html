Our Research

Our research spans multiple areas, unified by a critical approach and a dedication to evidence-based social change.

Research areas
Restorative justice
We study public attitudes toward restorative justice and immigration policy, and the social-psychological predictors of support for equitable approaches to harm and accountability. Our current project uses path analysis to examine how social dominance orientation, mindfulness, empathic concern, and psychological essentialism shape policy attitudes. Following a completed pilot study, we are collecting data from a community sample of adults recruited through Prolific, an online research participant platform, in Fall 2026.*

*Work supported by a 2025-26 Psi Chi Mamie Phipps Clark Diversity Research Grant.

Work-nonwork dynamics
We investigate the intersection of work and nonwork roles, in pursuit of lives well-lived and organizations that thrive. Ongoing work in this area explores the relationship between educational experiences and the self-efficacy beliefs people carry into their work and life roles. Our current follow-up survey examines the work-life expectations of Gen Z and Gen Alpha college students, comparing them with earlier cohorts to track generational change (data collection Fall 2026).
AI and the Social World Emerging
We are interested in the reciprocal relationship between AI and society: how human values and choices shape AI’s design, and how these systems in turn reshape human behavior and institutions. Our current work draws on Tri-System Theory (Shaw & Nave, 2026), which extends dual-process models of human reasoning by adding artificial cognition as a “System 3” alongside fast, intuitive thinking (System 1) and slow, deliberative thinking (System 2). Building on this framework, we are designing a study of cognitive surrender: when and why people defer to AI-generated judgments, and how that reliance shapes accuracy, confidence, self-efficacy, and motivation (data collection Spring 2027).
